\begin{abstract}
We extend a previously verified finite H-Tilt survivor law from conditional
eigenmode algebra to a discrete-time Perron--Frobenius existence setting. The
original Lean theorem established that, given left and right modes \(q,h\)
satisfying eigen-equations, the biorthogonal weight \(q_i h_i\) is stationary
under a Doob-transformed operator. Here we add the discrete-time kernel
\[
  D_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}h_j}{\rho h_i}
\]
and verify its stationarity, nonnegativity, and row normalization under
explicit eigenmode and positivity assumptions. Using a quarantined exact-pin
Lean formalization of Perron--Frobenius theory, we then prove that every finite
irreducible nonnegative real matrix admits \(\rho>0\) and strictly positive
modes \(h,q\), obtained from \(A\) and \(A^\mathsf{T}\), for which the
normalized biorthogonal distribution is strictly positive and stationary.
Lean's axiom audit finds no \texttt{sorryAx} dependency in the promoted
theorem, although the external subtree is not claimed globally
placeholder-free. The result closes the eigenmode-existence obstruction in the
discrete nonnegative setting. Killed-generator bridges, convergence and mixing,
empirical h-band claims, consciousness, and scheduler performance remain
outside the theorem boundary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The finite H-Tilt development began with a small algebraic observation. If a
finite operator has compatible left and right modes, then their pointwise
product is the stationary weight naturally associated with the corresponding
Doob transform. The cancellation is elementary on paper. The formal value lies
elsewhere: Lean fixes which mode is used on which side, exposes every nonzero
denominator, and prevents the stationary identity from silently acquiring
existence, convergence, or empirical interpretations.

The first verified artifact deliberately assumed the modes. That left a named
obstruction: under what hypotheses do positive left and right modes exist?
Perron--Frobenius theory answers this question for finite irreducible
nonnegative matrices. A recent Lean development supplies the required theorem
under a separate exact-pin environment. Rather than merge a large external
project into the main dependency graph, we isolate a small portal theorem and
audit the exact axioms on which it depends.

The contribution is therefore a theorem tower rather than one inflated claim.
The bottom layer is generator-style conditional algebra. The middle layer is a
discrete conditional stochastic package. The top layer invokes
Perron--Frobenius theory to discharge the middle layer's mode assumptions. Each
layer has its own environment and explicit non-claims.

\section{The Existing Conditional Survivor Law}

Let \(K\) be a finite real operator, \(\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\), and let \(h\)
and \(q\) satisfy right and left eigen-equations with eigenvalue \(\lambda\).
The original artifact defines a generator-style Doob entry from
\((K-\lambda I)\) and \(h\). It verifies zero row sums from the right equation
and stationarity of the weight
\[
  \widehat{\pi}_i=q_i h_i
\]
from the left equation. When \(Z=\sum_i q_i h_i\neq 0\), it also verifies the
normalized identity for \(\pi_i^*=\widehat{\pi}_i/Z\).

This statement is conditional. It neither constructs \(q,h\) nor shows that
they are positive. Moreover, \(K-\lambda I\) is not generally a nonnegative
matrix, so finite-matrix Perron--Frobenius theory cannot simply be applied to
the generator expression. This boundary motivates the discrete intermediate
layer.

\section{A Conditional Discrete Doob Kernel}

Let \(A\) be a finite real matrix and suppose
\[
  Ah=\rho h,\qquad A^\mathsf{T}q=\rho q,
\]
with \(\rho\neq 0\) and \(h_i\neq0\). Define
\[
  D_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}h_j}{\rho h_i}.
\]
For the unnormalized survivor weight, the stationary calculation is
\begin{align*}
\sum_i q_i h_i D_{ij}
  &=\sum_i q_i h_i\frac{A_{ij}h_j}{\rho h_i}\\
  &=\frac{h_j}{\rho}\sum_i q_iA_{ij}\\
  &=\frac{h_j}{\rho}\rho q_j\\
  &=q_jh_j.
\end{align*}
The right eigen-equation similarly yields
\[
  \sum_j D_{ij}
  =\frac{1}{\rho h_i}\sum_j A_{ij}h_j
  =1.
\]

Now assume \(A_{ij}\ge0\), \(\rho>0\), \(q_i>0\), and \(h_i>0\). Then
\(D_{ij}\ge0\), every survivor weight is positive, and the finite nonempty sum
\[
  Z=\sum_k q_kh_k
\]
is positive. Hence
\[
  \pi_i^*=\frac{q_i h_i}{\sum_k q_kh_k}
\]
satisfies
\[
  \pi_i^*>0,\qquad \sum_i\pi_i^*=1,\qquad
  \sum_i\pi_i^*D_{ij}=\pi_j^*.
\]
Lean verifies these component lemmas and a combined stochastic
stationary-distribution theorem. At this layer, however, \(\rho,q,h\) are still
inputs.

\section{The Perron--Frobenius Portal}

The portal starts with an irreducible nonnegative matrix
\(A:\mathrm{Matrix}\ \iota\ \iota\ \mathbb{R}\), where \(\iota\) is finite and
nonempty. The external Perron--Frobenius development yields a positive
eigenvalue and a strictly positive right eigenvector for \(A\). Irreducibility
is preserved by transpose, so the theorem applied to \(A^\mathsf{T}\) yields a
strictly positive transpose-right mode. A formal equality between the Perron
roots of \(A\) and \(A^\mathsf{T}\) aligns the two eigenvalues.

These outputs discharge every positivity and eigenmode hypothesis in the
conditional discrete package. The strongest portal theorem therefore
constructs \(\rho,h,q\) and concludes simultaneously that \(D\) is a
nonnegative row-stochastic matrix and that \(\pi^*\) is a strictly positive,
normalized stationary distribution.

The theorem is intentionally discrete. A killed generator can contain negative
diagonal entries. Connecting it to a nonnegative PF object requires an
additional verified construction---for example a sufficiently large shift, a
matrix exponential, or a resolvent---together with proofs that the relevant
modes and normalization are preserved.

\section{Discussion}

The mathematical cancellation displayed here is compact. Its value as a
formal artifact is the compositional boundary: the conditional algebra can be
reused without importing Perron--Frobenius theory, while the portal can
discharge its assumptions in exactly the operator class where the external
theorem applies. The split also reveals the next honest target. A future
killed-generator result should not alter the discrete theorem; it should add a
new bridge theorem with its own hypotheses and replay boundary.

This pattern is useful beyond H-Tilt. A speculative corpus may contain a small
exact identity embedded in much broader interpretation. Extracting that
identity, formalizing its assumptions, and recording what it does not authorize
creates a reusable verified kernel. Additional theory can then attach through
explicit portals rather than through rhetoric.
