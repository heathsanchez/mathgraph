\begin{abstract}
We formalize the finite-state algebraic core of an enhanced H-Tilt framework.
Given a finite real-valued operator \(K\), a scalar \(\lambda\), and left and right modes
\(q,h\) satisfying the corresponding eigenvector equations, the
Doob-transformed entries admit the normalized biorthogonal weight
\(\pistar_i \propto q_i h_i\) as a stationary weight. We verify this identity
in Lean, together with row-sum cancellation for the Doob transform, exact
containment of \(\pistar\) by the paper-native multiplicative bridge at
\(\beta=1\), and the positive-domain log-exp bridge identity. The result does
not establish Perron--Frobenius existence, empirical h-band universality,
consciousness, scheduling performance, Markov convergence, or bridge
optimality. Its contribution is a small verified kernel separating exact
survivor algebra from empirical and philosophical conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

H-Tilt originated as a heuristic reweighting idea within a larger speculative
corpus. That setting mixed several kinds of statements: finite algebraic
identities, numerical observations, proposed empirical regularities, scheduling
policies, and broad interpretations. A numerical replay can support an
identity, but it cannot promote that identity to a theorem; likewise, a theorem
about a finite operator cannot transfer authority to empirical or interpretive
claims. The work reported here isolates the part that survives those
distinctions.

The contribution is deliberately narrow. We formalize a finite index set, a
real-valued operator \(K\), a scalar \(\lambda\), and left and right modes
\(q,h\) satisfying explicit eigen-equations. From these data we define a
Doob-transformed entry and prove that the biorthogonal weight \(q_i h_i\) is
stationary. When its total weight is nonzero, the corresponding normalized
weight is stationary as well. The right eigen-equation independently yields
zero row sums for the transformed entries.

Although the cancellation is elementary, formalization serves a different
purpose: it fixes the exact hypotheses, distinguishes the uses of left and
right modes, separates two bridge definitions that were previously conflated,
and prevents empirical or interpretive claims from inheriting theorem status.

We also resolve a definition-level ambiguity discovered during formalization.
An early object called \texttt{powerBridge} was a geometric interpolation from
\(q\) to \(\pistar\), whereas the paper-native bridge normalizes
\(q_i h_i^\beta\). The Lean development now gives these constructions separate
canonical names and proves exponent-one containment for the paper-native
bridge. It also proves the positive-domain identity relating real powers to a
log-exp representation.

The proof is simple enough to display in full, while the associated Lawbook
record preserves both the verified statements and their non-claims. We use
``survivor law'' only as the artifact's established name; the proved object is
a stationary algebraic weight. Positivity and probability-distribution
semantics are not established here.

\section{Finite Algebraic Setup}

Let \(I\) be a finite type. Fix
\[
  K : I\times I\to\R,\qquad
  \lambda\in\R,\qquad
  h,q:I\to\R.
\]
Write \(K_{ij}=K(i,j)\), \(h_i=h(i)\), and \(q_i=q(i)\). The Kronecker delta is
\[
  \delta_{ij} =
  \begin{cases}
    1,& i=j,\\
    0,& i\ne j.
  \end{cases}
\]
Assume throughout the Doob quotient that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-nonzero}
  h_i\ne 0\qquad\text{for every }i\in I.
\end{equation}
Define the transformed entry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob}
  \Ltilde_{ij}
  =
  \frac{(K_{ij}-\lambda\delta_{ij})h_j}{h_i}.
\end{equation}
No sign condition on \(K\), \(q\), or \(h\) is included in the formal theorem.
Thus \eqref{eq:doob} is an algebraic Doob transform; identifying it with a
Markov generator requires additional hypotheses.

The left eigen-equation used for stationarity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left}
  \sum_{i\in I} q_i K_{ij}=\lambda q_j
  \qquad\text{for every }j\in I.
\end{equation}
For row-sum cancellation we separately assume the right eigen-equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right}
  \sum_{j\in I}K_{ij}h_j=\lambda h_i
  \qquad\text{for every }i\in I.
\end{equation}
These are assumptions, not existence conclusions.

Define the unnormalized biorthogonal weight, its total, and its normalized
form by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
  \widehat\pi_i=q_i h_i,\qquad
  Z=\sum_{k\in I}q_kh_k,\qquad
  \pistar_i=\frac{q_i h_i}{Z}.
\end{equation}
The normalized stationarity theorem assumes \(Z\ne0\). Neither \(Z\ne0\) nor
\eqref{eq:weights} alone makes \(\pistar\) a probability vector: nonnegativity
would still have to be supplied. The formal claim is a stationary-weight
identity.

\section{The Survivor Law}

\begin{theorem}[Finite H-Tilt survivor law]\label{thm:survivor}
Assume \eqref{eq:h-nonzero} and the left eigen-equation \eqref{eq:left}. Then,
for every \(j\in I\),
\[
  \sum_{i\in I}\widehat\pi_i\Ltilde_{ij}=0.
\]
If \(Z\ne0\), then also
\[
  \sum_{i\in I}\pistar_i\Ltilde_{ij}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finiteness makes every displayed sum algebraic. Substituting the definitions
and cancelling the nonzero \(h_i\) gives
\begin{align*}
  \sum_i q_i h_i\Ltilde_{ij}
  &=
  \sum_i q_i h_i
    \frac{(K_{ij}-\lambda\delta_{ij})h_j}{h_i}\\
  &=
  h_j\sum_i q_i(K_{ij}-\lambda\delta_{ij})\\
  &=
  h_j\left(\sum_i q_iK_{ij}-\lambda q_j\right)\\
  &=
  h_j(\lambda q_j-\lambda q_j)
  =0.
\end{align*}
The normalized statement follows by dividing the unnormalized identity by the
nonzero scalar \(Z\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Doob row-sum cancellation]\label{prop:rows}
Assume \eqref{eq:h-nonzero} and the right eigen-equation \eqref{eq:right}.
Then, for every \(i\in I\),
\[
  \sum_{j\in I}\Ltilde_{ij}=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using the finite delta sum and \eqref{eq:right},
\[
  \sum_j\Ltilde_{ij}
  =
  \frac{\sum_j K_{ij}h_j-\lambda\sum_j\delta_{ij}h_j}{h_i}
  =
  \frac{\lambda h_i-\lambda h_i}{h_i}
  =0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:survivor} and Proposition~\ref{prop:rows} use different
eigen-equations. The left equation yields stationarity; the right equation
yields row-sum cancellation. The Lean statements retain this separation.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Algebraic and probabilistic readings}

The terminology ``killed dynamics'' motivates the construction but is not
itself a formal hypothesis. A conventional probabilistic reading would add
sign, conservation, and normalization conditions ensuring that the relevant
operator generates a stochastic evolution and that \(\pistar\) is a
probability vector. Those conditions are absent from
Theorem~\ref{thm:survivor}. The theorem therefore remains valid in the broader
real-algebraic setting represented in Lean, including instances that would not
define a Markov process.

This distinction affects the word ``stationary.'' Formally, stationarity here
means annihilation under the displayed left action:
\(\sum_i\pistar_i\Ltilde_{ij}=0\) for each \(j\). It does not include
nonnegativity, unit total mass, irreducibility, uniqueness, or convergence from
an initial condition. If those properties are later supplied, the algebraic
identity can serve as one component of a probabilistic theorem; it is not a
substitute for the missing components.

The same caution applies to the mode terminology. Equations
\eqref{eq:left} and \eqref{eq:right} specify what \(q\) and \(h\) must satisfy
once given. They do not show that such modes exist, that they can be chosen
positive, or that they are unique up to scale. Isolating that conditional
kernel is useful precisely because any future existence theorem can target a
separate interface without changing the cancellation proof.

\section{Bridge Alignment}

The formalization distinguishes two bridge families. The geometric bridge is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric}
  G_\beta(i)=q_i^{\,1-\beta}(\pistar_i)^\beta.
\end{equation}
It interpolates algebraically from \(q\) to \(\pistar\) at the distinguished
exponents. The names \texttt{geometricBridge} and
\texttt{geometricLogExpBridge} now make this role explicit. Legacy names are
retained only as compatibility aliases.

The paper-native multiplicative bridge is instead
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplicative}
  \mu^{\mathrm{pow}}_\beta(i)
  =
  \frac{q_i h_i^\beta}{\sum_{j\in I}q_jh_j^\beta}.
\end{equation}
At exponent one, its numerator is \(q_i h_i\) and its denominator is \(Z\);
hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:containment}
  \mu^{\mathrm{pow}}_1(i)=\pistar_i.
\end{equation}
Lean verifies \eqref{eq:containment} for both a natural-exponent definition and
a real-exponent definition. In Lean's real field, division is totalized, so
the definitional equality at exponent one does not itself need a nonzero
denominator. Interpreting either side as a normalized law still requires the
domain condition \(Z\ne0\).

For real \(\beta\), strict positivity of \(h_i\) supports the pointwise identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logexp}
  q_i\exp(\beta\log h_i)=q_i h_i^\beta
  \qquad(h_i>0).
\end{equation}
This is an identity between two representations of the same paper-native
unnormalized factor. It is not an optimality statement and says nothing about a
preferred empirical range of \(\beta\).

The supporting delta lemmas reduce finite sums against \(\delta_{ij}\) to the
selected component. The main stationarity proof then follows the displayed
calculation in Theorem~\ref{thm:survivor}; the normalized theorem reuses that
result rather than reproving the cancellation.

The formalization uses functions \(I\to I\to\R\), rather than Mathlib's Matrix
API, to keep the trusted core small. The Kronecker delta is defined by decidable
equality on the finite index type. Finite sums are taken over the corresponding
\texttt{Fintype}; cancellation in the Doob quotient is discharged from the
explicit hypothesis \(h_i\ne0\). The normalized theorem additionally receives
\(Z\ne0\). No automation is used to infer either domain condition.

The bridge definitions are also separated at the API level. Canonical
\texttt{geometricBridge} declarations support the interpolation results,
whereas separate canonical declarations support the paper-native natural and
real bridges. Backward-compatible abbreviations preserve the earlier file's
theorem surface without making those abbreviations the source of the corrected claim.
This is a small example of a useful formalization outcome: proof checking can
expose not only a false step, but a mismatch between a mathematical name and
the object it denotes.

The verification boundary has three layers. The Lean compiler checks the
declarations against pinned dependencies. A source audit rejects proof
placeholders and target-specific axioms in the fixture. Repository tests then
check that theorem status, hashes, bridge labels, non-claims, and replay
commands agree across the proof, ledger, report, and Lawbook bundle. These
tests do not add mathematical authority beyond Lean; they protect the
packaging from drifting away from the checked artifact.

The artifact was produced by a short boundary-preserving pipeline:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{speculative corpus}\to\text{claim audit}\to\text{numerical replay}\\
\to\text{exact kernel extraction}\to\text{Lean proof}
\to\text{bridge correction}\to\text{Lawbook entry}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Numerical replay remained diagnostic. Only successful Lean checking supplies
the \(\verified\) terminal form.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

The value of the result is not mathematical novelty in the displayed
cancellation by itself. It is the combination of a compact identity, an
executable formal boundary, and a durable record of what the identity does not
authorize. That combination makes the kernel reusable without importing the
larger speculative context from which it was extracted.

Several extensions are natural but logically separate. A Matrix API
restatement could improve interoperability with existing Mathlib linear
algebra. A Perron--Frobenius development could supply existence and positivity
under appropriate hypotheses. Additional sign and conservation assumptions
could connect the algebraic transform to a genuine finite-state Markov
generator, after which convergence questions could be asked. Each is a new
theorem boundary.

Empirical work is separate again. Claims about shared viability geometry,
h-band calibration, pre-transition drift, or scheduling performance require
held-out data and frozen evaluation protocols. None follows from the present
proof. The verified kernel can be cited in such work as an exact algebraic
component, but it cannot serve as evidence for the empirical hypotheses.
